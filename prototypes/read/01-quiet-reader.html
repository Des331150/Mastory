What evaporation is

Evaporation is the process by which liquid water becomes vapour and leaves a surface. In a catchment it is the main way water leaves the system besides runoff, which is why every water-balance question starts by asking how much of it happened.

The lecture distinguishes three related processes, and the exam tends to test whether you keep them apart:

  • Evaporation: from open water and bare soil.
  • Transpiration: vapour leaving through plant stomata.
  • Evapotranspiration: the two together over vegetated land.

Your lecturer's note (slide 68): "Define the term before you use it." Definitions carry marks in this course.

Factors controlling the rate

The rate depends on energy supply and on how easily vapour is carried away. The deck lists six factors; four are examined most often:

  • Temperature: more energy, more vapour.
  • Wind: carries vapour away from the surface.
  • Humidity of the air above: the closer to saturation, the slower.
  • Area and depth of the water body.

Measuring it: the pan

Direct measurement uses an evaporation pan, a standard open container whose water-level drop over a day gives the pan evaporation. Because a pan is small and metal, it loses water faster than a real lake, so readings are corrected with a pan coefficient, typically between 0.6 and 0.8.

Worked example on slide 79 walks through the correction, it appeared as question 4 on the 2019 paper.
